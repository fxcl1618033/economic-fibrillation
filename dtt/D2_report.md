# D2 — robustness of the DTT escape model (simulation only)

Runs the plan committed before any D2 run (`dtt/D2_plan.md`, commit a4d6e36): Sid's recommended next simulation sequence plus a timing item. Code `src/ef/dtt_d2.py` and `src/ef/dtt.py`, seed 1310; `python -m ef.dtt_d2 run | rho | figures | report` (about 15 minutes on this Mac with 8 processes). Every number below is read from `dtt/results/d2_*.json`. One correction during the run: the constant-RMS control ignored ρ in the ρ sweep (it used σ√E[R²] for every ρ); fixed to each trial's RMS of its own ρ-weighted noise and the ρ sweep rerun (`rho` step). Nothing else used ρ < 1, and the reproduction (D0) is unchanged to every digit.

Base: N = 31, ω ~ Normal(0, 1), D = 0.02, μ = 0.2, σ = 0.82, T = 40, step 0.01, uniform phases at t = 0. Uncertainty: Wilson 95% intervals for rates; paired 95% intervals for differences between noise formulations on the same R(t) traces and Brownian increments. An effect *vanishes* where its interval contains 0.

## In short

- **Numerically sound.** The four step sizes agree to within about a point; the report's step (0.01) is at most 0.9 points from the finest (0.0025).
- **Coupling, measured within the model** (K against K = 0, same noise rule): tipping rises with K in 84 of 84 swept conditions. This is the fair measure of coupling, but most of it is built in: the noise is σR(t) by assumption, R rises with K, and escape rises with noise.
- **The report's 4.75% against 0.325% is not a coupling effect.** At K = 0, σR(t) tips in 4.6% of paths against 0.15% with independent noise, although the two have the same mean square noise (E[R²] = 1/N exactly). With each path's constant RMS it is 0.4%. The gap comes from R(t) fluctuating, and from its persistence: shuffling R in 1-unit blocks keeps most of it (3.0%), shuffling step by step removes it (0.6%). The ρ sweep shows it enters only through the shared part of the shock (K = 0: 0.5% at ρ = 0 to 5.3% at ρ = 1, constant RMS flat).
- **Time variation (σR(t) against each path's constant RMS) mostly survives.** Positive in 86 of 112 swept conditions, vanishing in 26, significantly negative in 0. Where tipping is neither rare nor certain (between 2% and 98%; 96 conditions) it vanishes in 16: D = 0.01, K = 1.87; D = 0.02, K = 1.87; D = 0.04, K = 1.87; D = 0.08, K = 1.87; ω s.d. = 0.75, K = 1.33; N = 21, K = 1.87; N = 31, K = 1.87; N = 61, K = 1.87; μ = 0.2, K = 1.87; μ = 0.3, K = 0.8; μ = 0.3, K = 1.33; σ = 0.82, K = 1.87; σ = 1.2, K = 1.33; T = 10, K = 1.87; T = 20, K = 1.87; T = 40, K = 1.87. Its size: up to 18 points; it shrinks to nothing as tipping saturates (K ≥ 1.87 at the base, large σ, μ = 0.3).
- **But 'time-varying R tips more' is not general.** Against the *ensemble* RMS (every path given the same average noise), σR(t) tips less at K ≥ 1.33 (73.9% against 82.9% at K = 1.333): spreading noise unevenly across paths helps when escape is unlikely and hurts when it is likely. The report's 32.9% against 20% (K = 0.8) is the first regime.
- **Order in time matters less.** σR(t) against R(t) shuffled in 1-unit blocks: positive in 56 of 112 conditions, vanishing in 56; at most 6 points.
- **Timing.** R rises over the last few model time units before an escape: at K = 0.8 it is 0.25 four units before, 0.32 two units before and 0.39 at passage, against 0.24 in paths not yet tipped; the rise is more than half done about 2.0 units before passage (K = 1.333: 2.0), a little longer than R's autocorrelation time (1.2 and 1.3 units). This is built in — the noise is largest when R is — and it is in model time; whether a lead of this kind survives annual sampling and the Paper 1 pipeline is the G1b question.

## 7. What survives, plainly

- The variance law (section A) is exact and is the informative core; it needs no simulation.
- 'More synchrony → more tipping' survives everywhere it can, but it is built in and says little.
- The time-variation effect (section E) survives in most conditions where tipping is neither rare nor certain, and is driven by persistent episodes of high R; it reverses against an ensemble-average control at high tipping rates.
- The section D comparison (4.75% against 0.325%) measures R fluctuating, not coupling.
- Synchrony leads escape in model time by a few units, by construction. The open question is the G1b one.
