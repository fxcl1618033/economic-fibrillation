## 01 · Method: from GDP to oscillators

How each economy's GDP becomes a phase oscillator and how the Kuramoto order parameter r(t) measures synchronization, with the first evidence that the measured synchronization is not chance. The four companion-book figures use Maddison real GDP per capita, the paper's data; one of them (F1.4) draws a series that matches no stored results file and should be redrawn. The six first-pass figures used World Bank GDP in current dollars, which mixes real output with inflation and exchange rates; they are kept as the record of the first run.

- **Status:** `current`
- **Files:** `figures/01_method/F1-01_pipeline_japan_real.png`
- **Shows:** (a) Japan's real GDP per capita rises smoothly; on its own it is not an oscillator. (b) The 2–9 year Butterworth band-pass (order 2, forward–backward) leaves the business-cycle wobble (red); its Hilbert partner (gold) is the same wobble shifted by a quarter cycle. ## 04 · What moves in a crisis: swing size, alignment and diversification

Three ways to split the jump in world output variance in a global recession: synchronization against swing size (amplitude), the exact identity |Z| = Ā × R_A (world swing = average national swing × amplitude-weighted alignment), and the variance decomposition into an own term (each economy's volatility) and a cross term (co-movement). The corrected reading: 82–83% (GDP-weighted) to 97% (equal-weighted) of the excess crisis variance arrives through the cross term, but within the cross term's rise, bigger swings contribute 46–50%, higher correlation 35–41% and their interaction 13–14%. So the defensible phrase is amplification transmitted by alignment. The older slogan 'correlation events, not volatility events', printed on F4.4 and F4.6, is wrong.

## 06 · Tipping tests: the honest nulls

Tests for the early-warning signature of a bifurcation (B-tipping): rising autocorrelation and variance of r(t) before crises, and slower recovery when the implied Kuramoto coupling is treated as the bifurcation parameter. All are null against 5,000 spectrum-preserving surrogate economy sets. In the Ashwin et al. (2012) taxonomy this points away from B-tipping and towards noise- or rate-induced transitions. A third variant (restoring time, csd_fast.py) was also null; its script and figure were lost with the September workspace.
