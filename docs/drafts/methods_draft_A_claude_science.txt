We use annual real GDP per capita from the Maddison Project Database, in constant 2011 international dollars (PPP-adjusted), for a fixed panel of

The panel is balanced: every economy has an uninterrupted annual observation from 1950 to 2022, so no interpolation, gap-filling or unbalanced-panel weighting enters the pipeline at any stage.

Two distinct date ranges are used deliberately and should not be conflated. It is a consequential choice rather than a formality: recomputing phases on a 1970–2022 series alone, with no run-in, changes the order parameter by up to 0.30 in individual years and moves the sign of one reported coefficient (§7.2).

4. Crisis dating
Crisis years are taken exogenously from the Kose–Terrones definition of a global recession, which identifies contractions in world real GDP per capita accompanied by broad-based declines in other measures of global activity. This yields five dates in our window: 1975, 1982, 1991, 2009 and 2020. These dates are fixed before any statistic is computed and are never adjusted, re-weighted, or selected on the basis of the synchronization series. We use only the crisis onset years; no crisis is assigned a duration.

The count matters for interpretation and is stated plainly: five events in 53 years. Every crisis-conditioned test in this paper therefore rests on five degrees of freedom at most, and the leave-one-out analysis of §7.3 shows that the effective count for the pre-crisis result is smaller still.

6.6 Critical slowing down. We test the standard early-warning battery — rising lag-1 autocorrelation, rising variance, and rising restoring-time — on the pre-crisis windows. All three are reported as null in Results. We note here, as a design limitation rather than a finding, that annual data over 8-year pre-crisis windows gives these estimators very few effective degrees of freedom, and that a null result is therefore uninformative about whether critical slowing down is present in the underlying continuous-time system.
