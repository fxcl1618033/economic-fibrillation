## What Russia changes, in plain words

Written by Claude after the S31 run, for Sid's review. S31 is the main sample from 13 October 2026 (amendment 3); S30
is the comparison. Every number is in the tables below; section 25 lists every crossing.

**Nothing in the primary comparison changes sign or crosses p = 0.05.** Adding Russia moves the headline numbers by a
few hundredths and leaves every pre-registered reading as it was on S30.

- **The rise before recessions holds.** Lead-1 is +0.231 on S31 (p 0.019, the floor; S30 +0.217). With past data only
  it is +0.379 (p 0.038). Leaving any one of the 31 economies out keeps it at p ≤ 0.038. Its one weak spot remains:
  with past-only phases and China left out it is p 0.057. Lead-2 (+0.235, p 0.038) still fails past-only phases
  (−0.044). It still rests on 2009 and 2020 (without either, p 0.23 and 0.28).
- **No lag survives a multiplicity correction** (Benjamini–Hochberg q 0.094 for lead-1, lead-2 and after-2, as on S30).
- **The fall after recessions stays fragile, and now sits right on the line.** After-2 is −0.258 (p 0.057) on S31, as on
  S30 (−0.236, p 0.057). But on S31, leaving out any of 20 of the 31 economies, or computing Russia's phases from 1992, takes
  it to p 0.019–0.038 (20 of the 42 crossings in section 25, all in that direction), while it stays non-significant under W, N, E and C, at
  3–10 years and with growth rates. Read it as on the edge, not as stronger: it is still a Limitations item.
- **Swing size, diversification and the network** barely move: r–swing link +0.646 (S30 +0.641); co-movement share of
  the recession excess 84.3% GDP-weighted, 97.0% equal (S30 83.3%, 96.8%); correlation and swing size 46% and 40% of the
  cross-term's rise (S30 44% and 41%); network tightening 1.62× on both; phase-lead spread 0.546 years (p 0.0002).
- **The alignment share (D11) moves again**: 28.5% on S31 against 18.9% on S30 (39.3% on S26, 33.0% on S30 without
  Iran). More evidence that it is sample-sensitive; Results still builds nothing on it.
- **The variance of r** still rises before recessions (+0.438, p 0.031) without its autocorrelation rising (−0.152,
  p 0.79), and still rests on 2009 and 2020 (follow-up: without them +0.018, p 0.51). Under the edge-robust convention E
  it now crosses into significance (p 0.054 → 0.045).

**Russia from 1992 only** (its Soviet-era years removed): lead-1 +0.224 (p 0.019), lead-2 +0.229 (p 0.038), r–swing
+0.655. The back-cast years do not drive the arc or the swing-size link; the only change is after-2 (−0.254, p 0.038).

**Inside the economies.** Russia enters only the real GDP channel (its other series start after 1965). The past-only
lead-1 of real GDP synchronization (Global Macro Database) crosses into significance: +0.319 (p 0.038) on S31 against
+0.311 (p 0.057) on S30. The short-rate and investment leads still fail past-only phases.

**Smaller changes.** Russia lags the world cycle by 0.76 years. The Fiedler split regroups: Russia joins Japan, Korea,
India, Turkey, Norway, Denmark, Indonesia, Thailand, China, Saudi Arabia and Iran (12), against the 19 others; lead-1
inside blocs is +0.25 (p 0.019) and between them +0.07 (p 0.36). The 31 produce 81.1% of world GDP in 1970 and 80.0%
in 2022 (S30: 75.6% and 77.2%).
