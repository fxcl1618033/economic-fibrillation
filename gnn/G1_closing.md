# Closing G1 and G1b — what the simulations say about Paper 1

Written 15 October 2026 from `gnn/G1_report.md` and `gnn/G1b_report.md` (numbers from `gnn/G1/` and `gnn/G1b/`, generated by `python -m ef.gnn.g1b report`). After G1b Paper 1 is locked: no new tests; anything new is Paper 2.

## What G1 found

G1 built worlds of 31 simulated economies with known coupling — phase oscillators (Kuramoto) and phase-and-size oscillators (Stuart–Landau) on the real trade network — calibrated to the real cycles, and ran every Paper 1 test on them.

1. **The tests have the right size.** With no coupling at all, 17 of 20 tests in each kind of world reject at or below their correct rate. The exception that mattered, the swing-size term of the identity (13.8% and 18.9%), was an edge effect; the edge-matched null of amendment 4 fixed it and the real result stayed at the floor.
2. **Dating recessions from world output builds in some synchronization.** In uncoupled worlds that date their own recessions, the two-sided crisis-year and lead-1 arcs reject in 8.2%–8.9% and 8.5%–8.6% of worlds, about twice the correct 3.8%. The past-only lead-1 does not (2.3%–2.9%). This is why the paper's lead claim rests on the past-only version (L8).
3. **The cross-term test detects co-movement, not co-movement at recessions.** It rejects in 97.1% of trade-coupled Kuramoto worlds even with the real recession dates, which have nothing to do with those worlds (L9).
4. **No G1 world produced the past-only lead.** With recessions dated from each world's output it rejected in 1.7% of trade-coupled, 2.9% of uncoupled and 2.5% of forced Kuramoto worlds — never above chance.

## What G1b found

G1b added one mechanism to the same worlds: a recession is an escape from a stable state, and the shocks that cause escapes grow with synchrony (DTT, diversification-type tipping, Sid's report of 13 October 2026). Its verdict, exactly as pre-registered: **partly**.

- In trade-coupled worlds the past-only lead-1 rejects in 6.3% of worlds (5.0%–8.0%), above chance and above both controls (constant noise; synchrony shuffled in time).
- But only 6.3% of worlds reach the real p = 0.038, against the 20% required, and 1.3% reach the real correlation of +0.379.
- Without trade coupling the layer leaves no past-only lead (4.0% uncoupled, 3.2% with common forcing).

## What it means for Paper 1

- **The tests can be trusted to have their stated size**, with the two exceptions now handled (the edge-matched null; the past-only lead as the lead claim). The κ = 0 table goes into the Methods as a validation (§5.3).
- **The lead claim rests on the past-only lead-1**, because the two-sided arc is inflated by how recessions are dated (L8).
- **The loss-of-diversification arithmetic is arithmetic.** Years when cycles happen to line up have larger world swings, coupled or not; recessions dated from world output are such years even without coupling. The theory box says so.
- **The mechanism check is suggestive, not confirmatory.** A synchrony-driven escape layer on coupled worlds can leave a past-only lead the pipeline detects, and coupling is needed for it; but a lead as large as the real one is rare under it. The real result is either an unusual draw, or the real mechanism is stronger or different from the one simulated (for example feedback from recessions to the cycles, which G1b leaves out). Paper 1 reports this exactly and claims no mechanism from it.
- **The real lead still rests on two events (2009 and 2020).** Nothing in G1 or G1b changes that.
