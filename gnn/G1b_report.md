# G1b — does a synchrony-driven escape layer reproduce the past-only lead?

Pre-registered in `docs/preregistration_G1b_dtt.md` (draft 14 October 2026, Sid's choices as amendment 1 of 15 October 2026, committed before any run); code `src/ef/gnn/g1b.py`; results in `gnn/G1b/`. Generated by `python -m ef.gnn.g1b report`; every number below is read from those files. **G1b is the last test Paper 1 takes.**

## The verdict

**Partly reproduced** on the pre-registered primary setting (trade-coupled worlds at κ\*, world-level state, noise σ·r(t) from the true phases, μ = 0.2, refractory 2 years, 1 model unit = 1 year).

- **(a) passes.** The past-only lead-1 rejects in 6.3% of 1,000 worlds (95% interval 5.0%–8.0%); the lower bound is above 3.8% (2/53).
- **(b) passes.** Against constant noise the rate is +3.50 points higher (+1.89 to +5.11); against shuffled synchrony +3.40 (+1.70 to +5.10). Same worlds and same escape noise in each pair.
- **(c) fails.** 6.3% of worlds reach p ≤ 0.038, the real p; the threshold was 20%. With 53 circular shifts p ≤ 0.05 and p ≤ 0.038 are the same event, so (c) asks that the rejection rate itself be at least 20%. Only 1.3% of worlds reach the real correlation (+0.379); the mean past-only lead-1 correlation over worlds is +0.031.

In plain words: the escape layer makes recessions likelier when synchrony is high, so it does leave a past-only lead that the Paper 1 pipeline can see, more often than chance and more often than the same layer with the timing of synchrony removed. But the lead it leaves is small and rarely significant. A result the size of the real one (+0.379, p = 0.038) is an unusual outcome of the layer, about one world in 16 at that p and about one in 77 at that correlation.

**Some lead was expected by construction** (stated in the pre-registration): the layer raises the chance of a recession when the true r is high. It does: in the primary worlds the true r averages 0.530 in recession years and 0.493 the year before, against 0.427 in other years. What the test adds is how much of that survives annual sampling, 53 points, a handful of events and phases estimated from past data: little.

**Coupling matters.** Without coupling the same layer, calibrated the same way, does not reproduce the lead: the past-only lead-1 rejects in 4.0% (3.0%–5.4%) of uncoupled worlds and 3.2% (2.3%–4.5%) with common forcing only; the verdicts are *not reproduced* and *not reproduced*. Only trade-coupled worlds give a past-only lead above chance.

## What was run

- **Worlds.** The G1 Kuramoto worlds themselves: 1,000 per setting, the same seeds and chunks, so world k here is world k of G1. Checked: each world's own recession dates (G1's rule B, from its simulated world GDP) equal the dates G1 stored in every world of every setting (network: 1000/1000, null: 1000/1000, forcing: 1000/1000).
- **The layer.** dx = (μ + x − x³) dt/τ + σ r(t) dW/√τ, integrated beside the oscillators every 0.01 years from 1950 with its own noise; r(t) from the model's true phases. An escape (x reaches 0) resets x to the lower equilibrium and marks a recession year unless one was counted in the previous two years; only 1970–2022 count.
- **Calibration.** σ set so that the mean number of recession years in 1970–2022 is 5, on 500 separate calibration worlds per setting. Only the count is used, never the years. Every layer and control was recalibrated the same way (table below).
- **Tests.** Each world's pipeline r(t) under convention P (the two-sided arc) and C (past-only), computed once from its simulated GDP exactly as for the real data; the arc at every lag against 53 circular shifts, one-sided in the pre-registered directions.
- **Controls.** Constant noise (g = σ_c in every world and year) and shuffled synchrony (each world's r(t) with its one-year blocks permuted), both recalibrated; the plain G1 world with recessions dated from world output (rule B) as a reference.

## Limits of this test

- One mechanism, one family of worlds (Kuramoto on the saved G1 trade network). The Stuart–Landau worlds were not used (Sid: the calibrated G1 Kuramoto world).
- The layer does not feed back on the oscillators: an escape does not change any economy's cycle. In the real world a recession is part of the cycles it is measured from.
- The calibration fixes only how often recessions happen. The worlds' synchrony is calibrated to the real mean r, not to its variance or persistence, which is what a lead depends on.
- A *partly* verdict does not say that the real lead is produced this way; it says that this mechanism can leave a past-only lead the pipeline detects, but rarely one as large as the real one.
