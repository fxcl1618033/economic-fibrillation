# Economic Fibrillation: Business-Cycle Synchronization and the Loss of Diversification in Global Recessions

**Siddhant Shinde**

*Draft for review, 15 October 2026. Paper 1 is locked: no new tests.*

**Abstract.** When the business cycles of many economies line up, a shock common to all of them stops cancelling in
world output. We measure that alignment for 31 economies over 1970–2022, treating each economy's 2–9 year cycle in real
GDP per capita as a phase oscillator, and relate it to the five global recessions. Synchronization and swing size move
together (detrended correlation {{S31/P/amplitude#corr_detrended|c3}}). In recessions co-movement supplies
{{S31/P/diversification#gdp_mean/cross_fraction_of_excess|pct1}} of the extra variance of world output, with correlation
and larger swings contributing about equally: the 31 economies behave like about
{{dtt/results/d3_neff.json#S31/P/all years/neff|f1}} independent ones on average and like one in 2020. Their
correlation network has tightened {{@mst_ratio:S31|x1}} since the 1980s. With phases from past data only,
synchronization is higher in the year before recessions ($p =$ {{S31/P/conventions#C/arc/lead1/p|p}}), but this rests
on two events, 2009 and 2020. The variance of synchronization rises before recessions without its autocorrelation
rising, so there is no critical slowing down; as an interpretation, this fits noise-induced rather than
bifurcation-induced tipping. Simulated worlds with known coupling show which tests can be trusted. We make no forecast.

**Keywords:** business-cycle synchronization; global recessions; Kuramoto order parameter; diversification; tipping
points
